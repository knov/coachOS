Clear packages make it easier to sell, easier to deliver, and easier for clients to say yes. When a prospect asks what you offer, a well-designed package gives you a confident, specific answer. When a client enrolls, it sets clear expectations on both sides. This section is where you design and document your offerings so your business has a solid foundation to stand on.

## Package Design Principles

Strong packages are built on four principles. These aren't just good marketing — they shape the quality of the experience you deliver.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Defined Scope" icon="list-check">
    Be explicit about what's included and what isn't. Specify the number of sessions, their duration, how much between-session support is available, and any other touchpoints. Clear scope prevents misalignment and protects your time.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Clear Outcome" icon="bullseye-arrow">
    Every package should be oriented around a transformation or result. What does the client achieve, resolve, or become capable of by the end? A clear outcome makes your offer compelling and gives the engagement direction.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Right Duration" icon="calendar-clock">
    Match the length of the engagement to the complexity of the goal. A focused tactical challenge might resolve in six weeks. A deep identity or leadership shift might need six months. Mismatched duration creates frustration for both sides.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Transparent Pricing" icon="receipt">
    State your rate, your payment schedule, and your cancellation policy clearly. Transparency builds trust before the engagement even begins and filters for clients who are serious about investing in themselves.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Common Package Structures

There's no single right way to package your coaching. The structure that works best depends on your clients, your niche, and the kind of work you do. These four models cover most coaching businesses — use them as starting points, not constraints.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Single Session">
    A standalone session designed for clients who want focused support on a specific topic or who aren't ready to commit to a longer engagement. Single sessions work particularly well for warm leads who want to experience your coaching before enrolling in a full package, or for past clients returning with a targeted challenge. Because there's no ongoing relationship to build, the session needs to be well-structured and immediately valuable.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Short Engagement">
    Typically four to six sessions delivered over one to three months. This structure suits clients with a focused, well-defined goal — a career transition, a specific skill they want to build, or a decision they're working through. It's long enough to create meaningful movement and short enough to feel accessible for clients who are new to coaching or cautious about commitment.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Deep Engagement">
    Ten to twelve sessions over four to six months, designed for transformation-level work. This is the right structure when a client is navigating significant change — a leadership shift, a major life transition, or deep personal development work. The longer arc gives you time to build trust, go beneath the surface, and help the client sustain new ways of thinking and operating.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Ongoing Retainer">
    A recurring monthly arrangement with no defined end date, typically built around one or two sessions per month. This works best for clients who want continuous support, accountability, and a thinking partner over the long term — often senior leaders, entrepreneurs, or anyone operating in a fast-moving, high-stakes environment. Retainers create stable revenue and deep client relationships.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Documenting Each Package

For each package you offer, document the following fields. This becomes your internal source of truth — the reference you use when writing proposals, answering questions, and reviewing your offerings over time.

- **Package name** — what you call this offering
- **Target client** — who this package is designed for
- **Coaching outcome** — the transformation or result the package is built around
- **Number of sessions** — how many sessions are included
- **Session length** — the duration of each session
- **Between-session support** — email, voice messages, check-ins, or none
- **Investment** — the total price
- **Payment schedule** — upfront, split, or monthly
- **What's not included** — anything a client might assume is included but isn't

<Info>
  Document your packages here even if they're not yet public or fully refined. This section is your internal source of truth for what you offer — not a sales page. Getting it written down is the first step to making it real.
</Info>

## Reviewing and Updating Pricing

Your pricing is not a fixed number — it's a reflection of where you are in your practice. Review your pricing at least annually, and revisit it whenever you experience a significant shift in your market positioning, your level of experience, the demand for your work, or the results your clients are achieving.

The most common mistake coaches make is underpricing and then staying there. Building in a regular review keeps your rates aligned with the value you actually deliver.

<Tip>
  Your pricing reflects your value proposition. When you raise your prices, make sure your packages, your positioning, and the transformation you deliver are clearly articulated — so the investment feels obvious to the right client.
</Tip>
