Repeatable workflows are the backbone of a smooth-running practice. When you document how you do things, you can deliver a consistent client experience, reduce the mental load of figuring out each step on the fly, and free up energy for the work that actually requires you — the coaching itself.

## Core Workflows to Document

Start with the workflows that run most frequently or carry the most consequence if something is missed. The five below cover the essential rhythm of most coaching practices.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Client Onboarding">
    The onboarding workflow begins the moment a client says yes and ends when they're fully set up and ready for their first session. A strong onboarding process builds immediate trust and sets the tone for the entire engagement.

    **Steps to include:**
    - Send welcome email with next steps and what to expect
    - Share and collect the signed coaching agreement
    - Send intake form and review responses before the first session
    - Process payment and confirm payment schedule
    - Schedule the first session (and ideally all sessions in the package)
    - Set up the client's knowledge base and profile in Coaching OS
    - Send a welcome packet with any pre-work or orientation materials
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Session Preparation">
    Preparation is what separates a good session from a great one. A short, consistent pre-session routine ensures you arrive focused and ready — not scrambling to remember where you left off.

    **Steps to include:**
    - Review your notes from the previous session
    - Note any commitments the client made and whether they followed through
    - Review the client's stated goals and current lifecycle stage
    - Set an intention for this session
    - Prepare any materials, assessments, or frameworks you plan to use
    - Clear your schedule and environment for the session window
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Post-Session Processing">
    What you do in the hour after a session determines how useful your records will be weeks from now. Capture everything while it's fresh — the notes you skip will be the ones you need.

    **Steps to include:**
    - Log session notes using your standard template
    - Note key themes, breakthroughs, and commitments made
    - Send a session summary to the client (if this is part of your service)
    - Update the client's profile with any changes to goals or stage
    - Flag any follow-up actions on your end (resources to send, connections to make)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Monthly Business Review">
    The monthly review is your opportunity to step back from delivery and look at the health of your practice as a whole. It keeps small issues from becoming large ones and ensures you're making intentional decisions rather than reactive ones.

    **Steps to include:**
    - Review all active clients — progress, engagement, and upcoming milestones
    - Check revenue against your monthly target
    - Review capacity — are you at the right number of clients?
    - Note any workflows that caused friction and update them
    - Review your pipeline — any prospects to follow up with?
    - Plan the month ahead: sessions, admin, any marketing or outreach
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Client Offboarding">
    Offboarding is as important as onboarding. A deliberate close honors the work done, leaves the client feeling supported, and keeps the door open for future re-engagement or referrals.

    **Steps to include:**
    - Prepare a final session agenda focused on reflection and integration
    - Share a reflection worksheet ahead of the final session
    - Deliver the final session and close the engagement intentionally
    - Send a post-session thank-you and summary
    - Ask for a testimonial with a simple, clear request
    - Make a referral ask when the timing feels right
    - Create an alumni touchpoint plan — when and how you'll stay in touch
    - Archive the client's profile in Coaching OS with a closure note
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## How to Document a Workflow

If you've never written down a workflow before, start simple. You don't need special software or elaborate templates — just a clear, ordered record of how you do something. Follow these steps for any workflow you want to document.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Name the workflow and note when it triggers">
    Give the workflow a clear name and define the event that kicks it off. For example: "Client Onboarding — triggers when a client signs the agreement." This makes it easy to know which workflow applies in any given situation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="List every step in order">
    Write out every action required to complete the workflow from start to finish. Don't skip steps because they feel obvious — obvious steps are often the ones that get missed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Note who does each step">
    For each step, note whether it's done by you, a virtual assistant, or an automated tool. This makes delegation and automation easier when you're ready for it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="List any templates, tools, or resources needed">
    Identify the documents, links, software, or materials required at each step. Attach or link to them directly if possible so you never have to hunt for them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Note quality checks or decision points">
    Flag any steps where you need to verify something before moving on, or where the path forward depends on a client's response or a decision you need to make. These are the places where workflows most often stall.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Start by documenting the workflows you already do — capture the process you actually use, not an idealized version of it. Getting the real workflow written down first makes it much easier to improve.
</Tip>

## Keeping Workflows Current

Workflows are not set-it-and-forget-it documents. Your practice evolves, your tools change, and what worked six months ago may create unnecessary friction today. After completing any workflow, take thirty seconds to note any friction points — steps that took longer than expected, things that got missed, or handoffs that felt clunky.

Use your monthly business review as a standing time to revisit your workflows and make improvements. Small, regular updates keep your processes sharp without requiring a major overhaul.
