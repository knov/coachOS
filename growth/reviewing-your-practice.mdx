A coaching practice that doesn't review itself stagnates. Building a regular review rhythm into Coaching OS ensures your craft, your business, and your self-awareness continuously evolve rather than just accumulate. Reviews are where scattered experience becomes integrated wisdom.

## The Review Cadence

Different review intervals serve different purposes. Weekly reviews keep you present and responsive. Monthly and quarterly reviews reveal patterns and prompt adjustments. Annual reviews give you the altitude to see the full arc of your practice and set a deliberate direction forward.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Weekly">
    **Time investment: 15–30 minutes**

    Your weekly review is a brief, high-frequency check-in that keeps you connected to what's actually happening in your practice. It doesn't need to be comprehensive — it needs to be consistent.

    Cover these questions each week:

    - What happened in sessions this week? What themes or moments stand out?
    - What went well? What felt off?
    - What do I want to carry forward into next week?
    - Who do I have next week, and what prep do I need?

    The goal is to close the loop on the week before it blurs into the next one.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Monthly">
    **Time investment: 60–90 minutes**

    Your monthly review is where you zoom out from individual sessions and look at your practice as a whole. It's the right level for spotting patterns, catching drift, and making small course corrections before they become big ones.

    Cover these areas each month:

    - Review all active client progress — who is moving? Who is stuck?
    - Check in on business metrics: current client count, revenue, capacity utilisation
    - Scan your workflows for friction — what's taking longer than it should?
    - Capture insights from the month that belong in your system
    - Update any frameworks that have shifted based on what you've learned
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Quarterly">
    **Time investment: 2–3 hours**

    Your quarterly review is a strategic check-in on the direction of your practice. It sits above the operational detail and looks at whether you're heading where you want to go.

    Cover these areas each quarter:

    - Review your coaching philosophy and frameworks for continued relevance
    - Assess business performance against the goals you set at the start of the quarter
    - Review your own development progress — what have you done, what did you skip?
    - Identify what's working well enough to double down on
    - Set 1-3 focus areas for the next quarter
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Annual">
    **Time investment: Half-day or full day**

    Your annual review is a retreat. Take yourself away from your normal work environment if possible, and give yourself the space to look at the full year with honest eyes.

    Cover these areas in your annual review:

    - How has your coaching craft evolved this year? What's different about how you coach?
    - What happened in your business — growth, challenges, pivots, wins?
    - What were the most significant outcomes for your clients?
    - How did your personal development unfold? What did you invest in, and what did you neglect?
    - What do you want for the practice in the year ahead — in your craft, your business, your clients, and yourself?

    Set the vision and priorities that will anchor your quarterly reviews for the next twelve months.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Weekly Review Template

Use this template as a starting point for your weekly reviews. Copy it, adapt it to your own voice, and store completed reviews in a dedicated log so you can look back across time.

```markdown
## Weekly Review — Week of [Date]

### Sessions This Week
- [Client / Notable themes or moments]

### What Went Well
-

### What Felt Off or Challenging
-

### Insights About My Coaching
-

### What I Want to Carry Forward
-

### Next Week Prep
- Sessions: [list]
- Any special prep needed:
```

Keep your completed weekly reviews somewhere you can scan them easily. Patterns that are invisible week-to-week become unmistakable when you read five reviews in a row.

## Making Reviews Stick

The most common reason reviews fail is that they become aspirational rather than actual — something you plan to do when things slow down, rather than a fixed rhythm built into your operating system.

<Tip>
  Reviews don't have to be long to be valuable. A 15-minute weekly review beats a 2-hour monthly review you never do. Start smaller than feels sufficient, then expand once the habit is solid.
</Tip>

Block your review times in your calendar as recurring appointments. Give them the same status as client sessions — they are client sessions, in a sense, except the client is your practice. When something tries to displace your review slot, reschedule it rather than cancelling it. Over time, the compounding effect of consistent reflection becomes one of the most powerful investments you make in your work.

## Feeding Reviews Back into the System

A review that changes nothing is just journaling. The real value of your review cadence is in what it feeds back into Coaching OS — updated frameworks, revised workflows, sharper development priorities, and new insight captured before it fades.

After every review, ask: what in my system should change based on what I just noticed? Then make that change. Even a small update — a revised question in a framework, a new step in a client onboarding workflow, a book added to your development plan — compounds across hundreds of future interactions.
