One of the most important principles of coaching is confidentiality — and that principle extends to how you manage client information. Coaching OS gives every client a fully compartmentalized knowledge base: their own dedicated space that holds everything about that relationship, completely separate from every other client.

## What Each Client Knowledge Base Contains

Every client knowledge base is made up of three core sections. Together, they give you a complete picture of the client, the work you've done together, and the progress they've made.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Client Profile" icon="user" href="/clients/client-profiles">
    Intake information, goals, background, and contact details — the foundation of everything you know about this client.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Session Notes" icon="notebook" href="/clients/session-notes">
    A chronological record of every session — themes, shifts, commitments, and your observations as the coach.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Progress Tracking" icon="chart-line" href="/clients/progress-tracking">
    Milestones, goals, and the evolution of the client's thinking and behavior over the full engagement.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Why Compartmentalization Matters

<Note>
  Each client's information is held in its own distinct space. You never risk mixing up context, insights, or notes between clients — even when working with many clients at once.
</Note>

Compartmentalization also helps you prepare for sessions more effectively. When you open a client's knowledge base, you step into their world — their language, their goals, their open threads — without other client noise bleeding in. The result is a quality of presence and continuity that clients feel, even if they can't name exactly what creates it.

## Setting Up a New Client

When a new client comes on board, follow these steps to get their knowledge base ready before the first session.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a Client Profile">
    Fill in intake information, stated goals, relevant background, and lifecycle stage. This becomes the north star for the entire engagement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Link Their Package">
    Note which offering they're on, their start date, and their expected end date so you can manage the engagement timeline clearly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare for the First Session">
    Review their profile and note any questions you want to explore. Going into a first session with genuine curiosity — grounded in what you already know — sets a strong tone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log the First Session">
    Open Session Notes and capture the first entry right after the session ends. The first session often surfaces themes that will run throughout the entire engagement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Begin Progress Tracking">
    Set up their initial goals and baseline so you have a clear starting point. You can't measure distance traveled without knowing where the journey began.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Managing an Active Roster

The value of a client knowledge base grows with every entry you add. Keeping each record current — logging sessions promptly, updating goals as they evolve, noting patterns as they emerge — means your system does real work for you between sessions.

A well-maintained knowledge base makes every session more powerful. You arrive prepared, you remember what matters, and you can see the full arc of a client's journey rather than just the most recent conversation. Over time, this continuity becomes one of the most tangible things you offer as a coach.

<Tip>
  The 15 minutes you spend logging a session right after it ends are the most valuable documentation investment you can make. Do it before you move on to anything else.
</Tip>
