A client profile is the foundation of each client's knowledge base. It holds the essential context you need to coach them well — their situation, goals, preferences, and where they are in their journey with you. The more intentionally you build and maintain this profile, the more prepared and present you'll be in every session.

## What to Include in a Client Profile

A complete profile covers the full picture of who your client is, what they came for, and how they work best. Use the sections below as your guide.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Basic Information">
    Name, contact details, how they found you, which package they're on, and their start date. This is administrative but essential — don't skip it even if the engagement is informal.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Background and Context">
    Their professional role, life situation, and any relevant history they've shared. This section gives you the frame for understanding their goals and the challenges they're working through.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Stated Goals">
    What they came to coaching for — captured in their own words where possible. Their language matters. The way a client names their goals tells you a great deal about how they see themselves and what they believe is possible.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Communication and Style Preferences">
    Preferred session format (video, phone, or in-person), preferred communication channel between sessions, and feedback style. Knowing how a client receives challenge and reflection helps you calibrate how you show up.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Assessments and Instruments">
    Any formal assessments used during the engagement — personality profiles, strengths inventories, 360 feedback — along with key results and your interpretations. These become reference points throughout the work.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Lifecycle Stage">
    Where the client currently sits in their journey with you: **Discovery**, **Enrolled**, **Active**, **Closing**, or **Alumni**. Keeping this field current makes roster management and business reporting significantly easier.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Creating a Profile

Set up a new client profile as soon as someone moves from prospect to enrolled. Don't wait until the first session — you'll want this ready before you meet.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open a new client space in Coaching OS">
    Give it the client's name and set the lifecycle stage to **Enrolled** right away.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the Basic Information section">
    Don't leave this blank even if the engagement is informal. Contact details, package, and start date take two minutes to fill in and save you time later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in Background and Context">
    Draw on notes from the discovery call or their intake form. Capture what feels most relevant to their coaching goals — you can always add more as you learn.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record their Stated Goals in their own words">
    This becomes the north star for the engagement. Resist the urge to rephrase or tidy up their language — the raw version is often the most useful.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Note Communication Preferences before the first session">
    A quick check before the first session — format, channel, feedback style — sets you up to work with this client in the way that works best for them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Keep your client profile as a living document. Update it as you learn more about them — as goals evolve, as context shifts, and as new patterns emerge across the engagement.
</Tip>

## Using the Profile in Sessions

Before every session, briefly review the profile — especially their stated goals, any recent notes, and open items. This 3-minute habit dramatically improves session quality. You arrive grounded in what this client is working toward, and you can notice in real time when the conversation is moving toward or away from what matters most to them.

The profile also gives you a reference point when a client loses sight of how far they've come. You can surface their original words back to them — which is often far more powerful than any reframe you could offer.

## Keeping Profiles Current

<Info>
  Update the Lifecycle Stage field as clients move through the engagement. This makes your roster management much easier and gives you an accurate picture of your business at any point in time.
</Info>
