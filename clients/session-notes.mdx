Session notes are the memory of your coaching relationship. A well-kept record lets you track a client's evolution, pick up exactly where you left off, and notice patterns across months of work together. They also protect the quality of your coaching — when you know you'll write things down, you pay a different kind of attention during the session itself.

## What to Capture in Session Notes

Good session notes cover more than just what was said. They capture the quality of the conversation, the moments that mattered, and your own internal experience as the coach.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Session Context" icon="calendar">
    Date, session number, format, and the client's energy or state at the start of the session. This sets the frame for everything that follows.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Key Themes" icon="tags">
    The main topics and questions that emerged — what the client brought, what surfaced unexpectedly, and where the conversation found its energy.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Insights and Shifts" icon="lightbulb">
    Moments of realization, mindset changes, and breakthroughs. These are the entries you'll return to when you want to show a client how far they've traveled.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Commitments and Actions" icon="circle-check">
    What the client said they would do before the next session. Capture their exact words — ownership of language matters for follow-through.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Coach Observations" icon="eye">
    Your own impressions, questions that arose for you, and what you noticed but didn't say. This is your private coaching space — be honest here.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Follow-Up Items" icon="list-check">
    Things to check on next time, open threads you want to return to, and questions you want to hold for a future session.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## A Session Notes Template

Use this template as your starting point for every session. Copy it into the session notes section of the client's knowledge base and fill it in right after the conversation ends.

```markdown
## Session [#] — [Date]

**Format:** [Video / Phone / In-person]
**Client State at Open:** [Brief note]

### Key Themes
-

### Insights and Shifts
-

### Commitments and Actions
- [ ]

### Coach Observations
-

### Open Threads / Follow-Up
-
```

Feel free to adapt the template over time — add sections that matter for a particular client, or trim ones that don't fit your style. What matters is that you use it consistently.

## When to Write Your Notes

<Tip>
  Write session notes within 30 minutes of the session ending. Memory fades fast — especially the nuanced stuff that matters most. The exact phrase a client used, the moment the energy shifted, the thing you almost said — these details disappear quickly.
</Tip>

Make a habit of capturing your Coach Observations separately from client-facing content. What you noticed but didn't say is often as significant as what was said aloud. These private notes are where your instincts live — and reviewing them over time makes you a sharper coach.

## Using Notes to Prepare for the Next Session

Your previous session notes are your best preparation tool. Before every session, run through this sequence:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the most recent session note">
    Read it fully before you do anything else. Let it bring you back into this client's world.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review their commitments">
    Note what they said they would do. You're not there to hold them accountable — but you are there to be curious about what happened and what it means.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check open threads">
    Look at the Follow-Up section from last time. Are there questions you wanted to return to? Themes that felt unfinished?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Note what you want to explore">
    Write down one or two things you're curious about or want to hold space for — not as an agenda, but as an intention.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set your session intention">
    Before you open the call, take a moment to arrive. What do you want to bring to this session as a coach?
  </Step>
</Steps>

For guidance on how to build this preparation practice into a repeatable session structure, see [Session Structure](/coaching-craft/session-structure).
