A well-defined session structure is one of the most practical gifts you can give yourself as a coach. When you're not thinking about what to do next, you can give your full attention to the client — to what they're saying, what they're not saying, and what the session actually needs in this moment. A default structure doesn't constrain the work; it frees you to be fully present within it.

## Anatomy of a Coaching Session

Every coaching session moves through a natural arc. These six phases give that arc a clear shape — from the preparation you do before the client arrives, through the work itself, to the documentation you capture afterward.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pre-Session Preparation">
    Before the session begins, take five to ten minutes to review your notes from the previous session, revisit any commitments the client made, and re-read their profile for relevant context. Then set your own intention: what kind of presence do you want to bring into this session? This brief preparation ritual shifts you from your day into your coaching role.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Opening / Check-In">
    The opening creates safety and helps the client transition from wherever they've just come from into the space of the coaching conversation. A simple check-in question — "How are you arriving today?" or "What's most alive for you right now?" — accomplishes this shift. Listen not just to the content but to the energy: what the client leads with often tells you as much as what they say.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Agenda Setting">
    Co-create what success looks like for this session before diving into the work. Ask the client what they most want to focus on, what would make this conversation feel worthwhile, and how they'll know they've gotten value. This step keeps the session anchored to the client's priorities and surfaces alignment — or useful tension — between what they want and what you noticed from your pre-session review.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Core Coaching Work">
    This is the main body of the session: exploration, challenge, insight, reframe, and planning. You're moving the client through a process of discovery — helping them see their situation more clearly, question assumptions, access their own wisdom, and move toward clarity or committed action. Stay in the client's agenda. Follow the energy. Use your frameworks and instincts in service of what this particular client needs right now.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Closing / Integration">
    Reserve the final ten to fifteen minutes for integration. Invite the client to synthesize their own insights: what are they taking away? What shifted? What do they want to commit to before the next session? Acknowledge the client and the work they did. End with clear, specific commitments — not vague intentions — so both of you leave knowing what comes next.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Post-Session Documentation">
    Capture your notes immediately after the session, while the conversation is still fresh. Record key themes and topics covered, notable shifts or breakthroughs you observed, your own coaching observations and instincts, and any follow-up items or commitments. This documentation becomes the foundation for your next pre-session preparation and the ongoing client record. See [Session Notes](/clients/session-notes) for how to structure and store this information.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Customizing Your Structure

The session arc above is a default — a starting point you adapt to the client, the moment, and the type of engagement. These time breakdowns are a practical starting reference for each session length you offer.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="60-Minute Session">
    | Phase | Suggested Time |
    |---|---|
    | Pre-Session Preparation | 5–10 min (before session) |
    | Opening / Check-In | 5 min |
    | Agenda Setting | 5 min |
    | Core Coaching Work | 35 min |
    | Closing / Integration | 10 min |
    | Post-Session Documentation | 10–15 min (after session) |

    In a 60-minute session, protect the closing. It's easy to let the core work run long and shortchange integration — but the closing is where the client consolidates what they've discovered and commits to action. Keep it.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="90-Minute Session">
    | Phase | Suggested Time |
    |---|---|
    | Pre-Session Preparation | 10 min (before session) |
    | Opening / Check-In | 5–10 min |
    | Agenda Setting | 5–10 min |
    | Core Coaching Work | 55 min |
    | Closing / Integration | 15 min |
    | Post-Session Documentation | 15 min (after session) |

    A 90-minute session gives you room to go deeper into the core work and allows for a more expansive closing. You can use the extra time to explore multiple facets of the client's situation, or to slow down and stay with something important longer than a 60-minute session allows.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Discovery Session">
    A discovery session has a different purpose — it's about mutual fit assessment, not coaching work. The structure reflects that.

    | Phase | Suggested Time |
    |---|---|
    | Welcome & Context-Setting | 5 min |
    | Understanding the Client's Situation | 20 min |
    | Sample Coaching (optional) | 15 min |
    | Explaining Your Approach | 10 min |
    | Questions & Next Steps | 10 min |

    In a discovery session, you're both evaluating fit. Come prepared to ask about what the client most wants to change, what's at stake, and what they've already tried. Be transparent about your philosophy, how you work, and what a coaching engagement with you involves. End with a clear next step — not a vague "let me know."
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Tip>
  Your structure will vary by client and by the energy of the moment. Some sessions need a long, slow opening; others jump straight into urgent work. The goal of having a default structure is not to follow it rigidly — it's to give you a reliable starting point you can deviate from intentionally, rather than losing your footing by accident.
</Tip>

## What to Prepare Before Every Session

Consistent pre-session preparation is a professional discipline that pays compound interest over time. Before each session:

- **Review your session notes from last time** — what were the themes, the commitments, the open threads?
- **Re-read the client profile** — refresh your picture of this person, their context, and their longer-term goals
- **Check on any commitments they made** — what did they say they would do? Do you know whether they did it?
- **Note any patterns you've been tracking** — is there something recurring across sessions that's worth naming today?
- **Set your own grounding intention** — take a breath, release the previous conversation or task, and decide how you want to show up for this client

This preparation doesn't have to take long. Even five focused minutes creates a meaningful difference in the quality of presence you bring to the opening of the session.
