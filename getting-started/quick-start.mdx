Coaching OS is your personal operating system for running a thoughtful, organized coaching practice. It brings together your coaching craft, business operations, and client knowledge into one coherent home — so nothing slips through the cracks and your best thinking is always within reach. This guide walks you through the four essential steps to get your system up and running in about ten minutes.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Orient Yourself">
    Before you start adding content, take a moment to understand how Coaching OS is structured. The system is built around three pillars:

    - **Coaching Craft** — Where you document your philosophy, frameworks, session structure, and the tools that shape how you coach.
    - **Business Operations** — Where you manage the lifecycle of your practice, from pricing and packages to client workflows and processes.
    - **Client Knowledge Bases** — Where each client gets their own dedicated space for intake information, session notes, and progress tracking.

    Browse through the sidebar to see each section. You don't need to fill everything in right now — just get a feel for where things live.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Document Your First Framework">
    Your frameworks are the backbone of your coaching approach. Head to **Coaching Craft → Frameworks** and create an entry for your primary coaching framework.

    Capture at least these three things:

    - **Name** — What do you call this framework?
    - **Purpose** — What problem does it help your clients solve?
    - **Key Phases** — What are the main stages or steps a client moves through?

    Even a rough first draft is valuable. You can refine it over time as your thinking evolves.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Set Up a Client Profile">
    Head to **Client Knowledge Bases** and create a profile for your first — or most current — client. A good profile captures the essentials from your intake process:

    - The client's coaching goals and what success looks like for them
    - Relevant background context you gathered during intake
    - Any agreements, focus areas, or boundaries you've established together

    This profile becomes the anchor for everything you'll track about this client going forward.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Log a Session Note">
    After your next coaching session, come back and capture it in **Session Notes**. A useful session note doesn't need to be long — aim to record:

    - **Date** of the session
    - **Key themes** that emerged in the conversation
    - **Follow-up actions** — commitments you or your client made

    The habit of logging session notes is what transforms Coaching OS from a static document into a living record of your practice.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  You don't need to build this system all at once. Start with the one section most relevant to where you are right now — whether that's a client profile, a framework, or a business process — and let the rest grow organically. A small, consistent system beats a perfect one you never finish building.
</Tip>
